Compatible Devices

Satellite radio works in more places than most people realise. Here's the full picture.

Advertisement

In Your Car

This is where most people use satellite radio. The car is genuinely the best use case for it — the nationwide coverage means you never lose your station on a long drive, and the commercial-free music is a real improvement over FM during a commute.

Factory-Installed Receivers

The majority of new vehicles sold today come with satellite radio hardware already built in. The antenna is integrated into the roof or windshield, and the controls are built into the factory stereo. If you bought a new car in the last several years, it almost certainly has this.

When you buy a new vehicle, you typically get a complimentary trial period — usually three months — already activated. After that you'll need to subscribe if you want to continue. The good news is that activating the subscription is quick and doesn't require any installation.

Aftermarket Receivers

If your car doesn't have built-in satellite radio capability, you can add it with an aftermarket receiver. These are standalone units that typically mount on your dashboard or console. The audio connects to your existing stereo via an auxiliary input or FM transmitter, and a small external antenna mounts on the outside of your vehicle.

The setup takes about 30 minutes for most people and doesn't require professional installation. Aftermarket receivers are available at electronics and automotive retailers.

Factory-installed satellite radio is standard on most new vehicles.

If you're not sure whether your car has satellite radio built in, check the stereo for an "SAT" button or look in your owner's manual. Most vehicles manufactured after 2015 have the hardware installed.

The streaming app brings satellite radio to any internet-connected device.

On Your Phone

The streaming app is available for both iOS and Android devices. It gives you access to the full channel lineup — the same channels available on your in-car receiver — plus on-demand content and some additional features not available on the satellite signal alone.

This is particularly useful for listening when you're not in your car. You can continue a show you started on the drive home, listen at your desk, or use it while exercising. The audio quality over a decent internet connection is excellent.

If you don't have a compatible car receiver and don't need one, a streaming-only subscription gives you the full music and talk lineup at a lower monthly rate than plans that include satellite reception.

Other Ways to Listen

💻

Web Browser

Log in on any computer and stream directly in your browser. No app installation required. Works on Mac, Windows, or any modern browser.

🏠

Home Receivers

Dedicated home radios and dock-and-play units connect to your existing home audio system and use the satellite signal — no internet required.

📺

Smart TV App

The streaming app is available on several smart TV platforms and streaming stick devices, so you can listen through your television's speakers or home theatre.

🔊

Smart Speakers

Ask a compatible voice assistant speaker to play a satellite radio channel by name. Works with most major smart speaker platforms.

🎮

Gaming Consoles

The streaming app is available through the app stores on major gaming consoles — a convenient option if your console is already connected to your TV and sound system.

🎧

Headphones & Wearables

If your wireless headphones or smartwatch connect to your phone, you can listen through the app without needing to hold your device.

🚢

Marine & Aviation

Specialised receivers are available for marine and aviation use. Coverage extends over most of North America and some surrounding ocean areas.

🚛

Trucks & RVs

Standard car receivers work in trucks and recreational vehicles. Larger vehicles may benefit from an enhanced antenna for optimal signal reception.

Getting Started Is Straightforward

If Your Car Already Has It

Check whether the satellite radio feature in your car is active. If you bought your vehicle new, there's a good chance a trial was activated at the dealership. If the trial has expired, you'll see a message on the radio screen indicating that a subscription is needed.

To reactivate, you'll need your Radio ID — a unique identifier specific to your receiver. You can usually find it by tuning to a specific channel number listed in your owner's manual, or by looking it up on the provider's website.

If You're Starting Fresh

If you're adding satellite radio for the first time, decide whether you want in-car satellite reception or just streaming access. If you want streaming only, download the app and create an account — that's it. If you want in-car reception in a vehicle without built-in capability, you'll need to purchase and install an aftermarket receiver first.

Either way, the subscription process itself is quick — just choose a plan, provide payment details, and your access is activated within a few minutes.
